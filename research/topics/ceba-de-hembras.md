# Ceba de hembras — is the female strategy sound?

> Living doc. Sources cited as `[S#]` → see [`../sources.md`](../sources.md).
> Directly relevant: Sabaleticas is 96% female and losing money.

## The honest answer: defensible, but structurally lower-margin

A *ceba de hembras* fights **two price headwinds at once**:

1. **Heavier = cheaper per kg** (the general ceba trap): levante ~$10,013/kg > gordo ~$9,021/kg (mid-2025). `[S4]`
2. **Female discount**: hembra de primera ~$7,115 (Nov 2024) to ~$7,843/kg vs. macho gordo ~$8,000–9,021/kg — roughly a **10–15% discount**. The market does discount females. `[S5][S4][S10]`

Plus a **performance headwind**: females typically gain less and finish lighter —
~400 g/day → ~360 kg vs. males ~500 g/day → ~480 kg. Fewer kilos, slower, sold
cheaper per kilo. `[S10]`

So the margin can **only** come from a wide **buy-spread on cheap females** + **cheap
kilos of gain** — never from the sale price.

## Where ceba de hembras *does* make sense `[S10][S8]`

- Females are **cheaper to buy and more available** — if your purchase channel gets a wide enough buy-spread, the cheaper entry can offset the cheaper exit.
- **Strong female demand exists**, just at lower $/kg: cull cows / *vacas de descarte* feed the large "asadero" market (e.g. ~75% of Orinoquía regional consumption is cull females/heifers for asaderos).
- You avoid competing for scarcer, pricier males.

## The strategic question for Sabaleticas

With a 96%-female herd and monthly losses: **is the female buy-spread in our actual
purchase channel large enough to beat the 10–15% sale discount *and* the slower/lighter
gain?** We can't answer that without our real purchase data.

**Sequencing:** pasture/carga/turnover dominate the math and are fixable cheaply — fix
those first. *Then* run the male-vs-female spread on real purchase/sale records before
deciding whether to switch the buying strategy. Don't switch on theory. `[S8]`

## Born-on-farm calves: sell at weaning vs. retain `[S8][S4]`

- Retaining is a long capital lock-up (males ~3 yr to market; females ~4 yr to first calving).
- The **levante phase often has the best $/kg** (levante > gordo). So retaining calves through *levante* (not all the way to gordo) can be the higher-margin window — **if** pasture supports cheap gain.
- Given pasture is currently the binding constraint, **selling at weaning is the lower-risk default until pasture/carga are fixed.**

## Open questions to research / measure

- Current Antioquia female price series specifically (figures above are national snapshots `[S4][S5]`).
