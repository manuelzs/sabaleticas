## History & land tenure

Sabaleticas was part of **Hacienda El Guaico**, the original (much larger) ranch, **split
twice** — both times north–south, *not* along the river:

1. **~40 years ago (~1986):** El Guaico split in **two** — the **northern** piece carved off
   (now Sabaleticas's north boundary).
2. **~20 years ago (~2006):** the remaining (southern) El Guaico split in **four** among
   siblings. **Sabaleticas is one of the four parcels** (~200 ha); the parcel that kept the
   name **"El Guaico" is now the southern neighbor**.

Per the owner: the parcels on the **east bank of the Poblanco are family** (the sibling
shares and neighbors on this side); the land **across the river to the west is not family**.
So the river is the western edge and a family/non-family line, but it is **not** one of the
El Guaico split lines — those run north–south.

## Water — no longer a future topic

The 2003 document is in fact a ***plano de aguas***: it maps a **piped distribution network**
(`---- Tubería`) fed by **nacimientos and a manantial**, running to a **represa (0.64 ha)**
and to **tanks by the house**, with tees and a ventosa along the line. Plus the **Río
Poblanco** on the west edge and the quebradas **Sabaletica**, **Buenavista** and **Cascajón**
crossing the farm. Details in [`plano-2003.md`](plano-2003.md).

⚠️ Per Manuel (2026-08-21) **this map of the water system is incomplete** — it's the best
information gathered so far — and the farm has since added **substantial reservoir-tank
storage in several places** that isn't drawn.

In *tierra caliente* verano, water is usually the real cap on carrying capacity — which
potreros have water decides which are usable when. With a hard dry season expected, this is
now **its own workstream**: [`../water/README.md`](../water/README.md).
