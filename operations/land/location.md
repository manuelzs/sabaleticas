## Boundary so far (confirmed by owner)

The farm is the band **between the road and the river**:

- **East edge:** **Ruta Nacional 25** (Vía La Pintada–Santa Bárbara). The **entire farm lies
  west of the main road** — so the road is effectively the eastern boundary, with the
  entrance fronting it.
- **West edge:** the **Río Poblanco**, which runs along the west. The farm sits on the
  **east bank** of the Poblanco.
- **North & south edges:** shared internal lines with the **sibling parcels** (see history) —
  not yet pinned; come from the boundary map.

## History & land tenure

Sabaleticas was once part of **Hacienda El Guaico**, the original (much larger) ranch, which
has been **split twice**:

1. **~40 years ago (~1986):** El Guaico split in **two**, divided by the **Río Poblanco**.
   The land on the **far (west) bank of the Poblanco** was carved off then — those owners are
   **not family**.
2. **~20 years ago (~2006):** the near half (east bank of the Poblanco) split again, among
   **4 siblings**. **Sabaleticas is one of those four parcels** (~200 ha).

So the **Poblanco is the family / non-family line**: **every parcel on the east bank is
family** (the four sibling shares — the adjacent "Hacienda El Guaico," Casa La Esmeralda,
etc.); **everything across the river to the west is not.** Our western edge (the Poblanco) is
therefore both a natural and a tenure boundary; our north/south lines are the ~2006 four-way
internal subdivision, which the boundary map should reconcile.

## Water (future topic — flagged by owner)

Water management is a **discussion to have later**. Two features matter: the **Río Poblanco**
along the western edge, and the apparent **reservoirs** inside the property. In *tierra
caliente* dry-season (verano) water is usually the real cap on carrying capacity — which
potreros have water decides which are usable when. When the potrero map arrives we'll locate
the water sources (river access, reservoirs, troughs, aqueduct) per paddock and fold it into
grazing planning.
