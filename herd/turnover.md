# Turnover — is the herd cycling or accumulating? (H1)

> Built only from data already in hand: the SINIGAN inventory ([`inventory.md`](inventory.md))
> and the GSMI sales cadence ([`movements.md`](movements.md)). **No prices, no weights needed.**
> This is the test of hypothesis **H1** in [`../financials/diagnosis.md`](../financials/diagnosis.md):
> *do cattle cycle through, or sit and accumulate while monthly costs run?*

## The short version

The herd structure shows **accumulation, not cycling.** ~142 hembras are at or past
finish age and the sale cadence is too slow to clear them. The exact head-sold-per-year
number is blocked by a data gap (below), but the *direction* is not in doubt.

## The age pyramid is top-heavy with finish-age females

From the SINIGAN roster (266 head, pulled 2026-05-21):

| Grupo etario | Head | Read as |
|---|---|---|
| Hembras 3–5 años | **7** | **Overdue** — past normal ceba finish age, still on farm |
| Hembras 2–3 años | **135** | **Finish cohort** — should be selling now |
| Hembras 1–2 años | 102 | Levante — the pipeline filling behind them |
| Hembras 9–12 meses | 10 | Likely born-on-farm |
| Hembras 3–9 meses | 1 | Born-on-farm |
| Hembras < 3 meses | 1 | Born-on-farm |
| Machos (all, 10) | 10 | Born-on-farm calves (4 at 2–3 yr → Central Ganadera) |

**~142 hembras (135 + 7) are at or past finishing age.** In a *ceba de hembras* these
are the inventory that should be turning into revenue. Two things stand out:

1. **The 7 hembras at 3–5 años are unambiguously overdue.** In a buy-fatten-sell model
   no female should still be on the farm at 3–5 years. They've stopped (or nearly
   stopped) gaining and are now just consuming pasture and burning their share of fixed
   cost. That is the accumulation failure made visible — animals that didn't leave when
   they were ready.
2. **135 head are stacked in the 2–3 yr bracket about to become the next 3–5 yr group**
   if cadence doesn't pick up. The pipeline behind them (102 at 1–2 yr) keeps filling, so
   the bulge isn't a one-off — it's structural.

## It's a velocity problem, not a capacity problem

Stocking rate: **266 head ÷ 200 ha = 1.33 animales/ha.** Against the research benchmarks
([`../financials/diagnosis.md`](../financials/diagnosis.md)): national "losing" carga is
~0.87/ha, a rotated target is 1–2/ha. So Sabaleticas is **not under-stocked** — the land
is carrying a reasonable number of animals.

The problem is *what* it's carrying: a backlog of finish-age females that should have
sold. Adequate stocking + slow turnover = **the pasture is full of money that isn't
moving.** This is exactly how H1 (low turnover) feeds H4 (subscale fixed cost): the same
land and labor bill is spread over too few completed sales, so fixed cost per finished
animal climbs.

## Sale cadence — frequent, but small and slow to clear the backlog

From GSMI ([`movements.md`](movements.md)): **75 valid sale guías over Jan 2025 – May
2026 (~16.7 months) ≈ 4.5 guías/month** — roughly a sale a week, in small lots
(single-digit to ~14 head). High *frequency* of *small* lots is the opposite of what
clears a 142-head finish backlog efficiently: you dribble animals out a few at a time
instead of finishing and moving batches.

## The turnover number itself — bracketed, because of one data gap

⚠️ **We cannot yet state head-sold-per-year precisely.** GSMI's `head` count lives inside
each guide's PDF and only **5 lot sizes were ever pulled** (10, 6, 14, 6, 1). The rest are
unknown. So any annual head figure is an assumption, not a measurement. Bracketed scenarios,
holding the herd ~steady at 266:

| If avg head/guía is… | → head sold/year (~54 guías/yr) | Implied herd residence | vs. target (≤12–14 mo to finish) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~2 | ~110 | ~2.4 yr | ~2× too slow |
| ~3 | ~160 | ~1.7 yr | slow |
| ~4 | ~215 | ~1.2 yr | near target |

The high-end sample average (~7) would imply selling more than the herd holds — impossible
in steady state — which itself tells us **most guías are small lots** (lots of 1–3 head),
consistent with the "small lots, forced single-animal sales" pattern in `movements.md`.
That pushes the real average toward the **slow** end of this table.

## What this confirms, and what it doesn't

- **Confirmed (structurally):** the herd is accumulating finish-age females faster than it
  sells them. H1 is real. The 7 overdue hembras + the 135-head bulge + adequate stocking
  are enough to say this without prices or weights.
- **Not yet quantified:** exact head/year, residence time, and therefore the precise size
  of the velocity gap. Two ways to close it:
  1. **Extract head-per-guía** from the remaining GSMI guide PDFs (re-pull when V6/V5 is
     up), or
  2. **One number from Manuel:** how many head did Sabaleticas sell in the last 12 months?
     Even a rough count collapses the table above to a single answer.

## So what — the lever

If turnover is the binding constraint, the highest-value action isn't buying more animals
or more land — it's **finishing and selling the existing finish-age cohort faster and in
better-priced batches** (ties to H6, the channel). Every month a finish-age female sits is
a month of fixed cost with no offsetting gain. **Start with the 7 overdue 3–5 yr hembras —
they should be on a sale list now.** But we confirm the magnitude with the head count
before sizing the prize.
