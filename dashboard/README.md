# Farm dashboard — project

> **Status: started 2026-08-21.** A working map exists today (below). Everything past
> "What works today" is a **plan, not a commitment** — captured so the idea isn't lost and so
> we can sequence it against the things that actually make money.

## The idea

One visual surface for the whole operation. Not a map with data bolted on — **the farm's
operational state, drawn on the ground it happens on.** Where the cattle are right now, what
grass is in each potrero, which tank is low, what's scheduled this week.

Manuel's framing (2026-08-21): *"a great visualization of the farm operations in general."*

Why it matters beyond looking good: this project has spent months unable to answer basic
questions — how long cattle sit, which potrero grows them, whether the rotation rests
properly — because **nobody writes it down.** A dashboard that makes recording a side effect
of looking is a records system that might actually get used. That is the real argument for
building it.

## What works today

`sabaleticas map` builds [`viewer.html`](viewer.html) and opens it. One file, off the disk —
**no server, no internet, no install, no mapping library.** Plain HTML canvas and vanilla
JavaScript, generated by [`../sabaleticas/map.py`](../sabaleticas/map.py) from
[`map.template.html`](map.template.html).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Basemap** | Our own IGAC **0.5 m orthophoto** — sharper than any tile service here, and offline |
| **Layers** | Linderos · cercas · drenajes · depósitos de agua · **curvas de nivel a 5 m y 25 m** · bosque · vías · construcciones · vecinos |
| **Cursor** | Latitude, longitude and **elevation** anywhere, from the IGAC terrain model at ~6 m |
| **Measure** | Distance, **elevation difference** and slope between clicked points — and whether water **flows by gravity or needs a pump** |
| **3D** *(optional toggle)* | The terrain, textured with the orthophoto. Drag to orbit, wheel to zoom, adjustable vertical exaggeration. **2D stays the primary view** |

Rebuild it whenever the geodata changes. Everything it draws is canonical GeoJSON in
[`../operations/land/geo/`](../operations/land/geo/) — the dashboard renders, it never owns
data.

## The blocker everything else waits behind

> ### There is no potrero layer.
> Grass type, animal location, head counts, spray schedules — **every single item below hangs
> off knowing where the potreros are.** Without polygons there is nothing to attach anything
> to.

IGAC's `Cerca` layer is **not** it: incomplete, many fences missing, several enclosures don't
close (Manuel, 2026-08-21). A skeleton, not a map.

Three ways to fix it, cheapest first:

1. **Trace them on the orthophoto.** Fence lines are legible at 50 cm — sharper than IGAC's
   own vector layer. Add a drawing mode to the viewer, trace, export GeoJSON, **areas
   computed from the geometry** rather than transcribed. Costs nothing but time.
2. **Manuel's updated plan**, when it arrives — digitize once, properly.
3. **Walk them with GPS** — most accurate, most effort; worth it only for paddocks in dispute.

**Recommendation: do (1) now.** It unblocks the entire project this week instead of next
quarter, and (2) can correct it later.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Potreros *(the unlock)*
Polygons, names, computed areas. Then attach what we already know or can observe: **grass
species**, water access, slope class (we have the terrain model — steepness per potrero is
computable today), forest cover.

Immediate payoff even with nothing live: **grazing-area-per-potrero and slope let us rank
paddocks**, which is the first real input the pasture plan has ever had.

### Phase 2 — Where the cattle are *(the one that pays for itself)*
Not sensors. **A person recording moves.** Group X into potrero Y on date Z, with head count.
A tap on a map, not a form.

This is the phase worth building, because it silently produces the records this whole
advisory has been blocked on:

- **days-on-potrero** → real rest periods, and whether the rotation is grazing grass too soon
- **days-on-farm per lote** → the turnover number in
  [`../financials/diagnosis.md`](../financials/diagnosis.md), still unmeasured
- **kg of gain per hectare per potrero**, once weights exist — which paddock actually grows cattle

Manuel's "every time they move them, we get a notification and we just move them" is exactly
right, and it's the cheapest phase here: no hardware, no connectivity, no recurring cost.

### Phase 3 — Sanitary & operational calendar
Vaccination, deworming, fumigación, pasture work — scheduled and shown against the potrero
they apply to. Overdue items visible on the map rather than buried in a notebook.

### Phase 4 — Live sensors
Manuel already has **cameras and sensors at the house reporting temperature** — the first
real-time feed available, and the natural pilot: one value, one widget, end to end.

Then the one that would genuinely change decisions: **tank and represa water levels.** Level
telemetry turns the verano question ([`../operations/water/README.md`](../operations/water/README.md))
from an annual guess into a live readout — how much buffer is left, how fast it's drawing
down, how many days of autonomy remain at current herd size.

**Manuel flags this as medium-term: likely expensive, and connectivity out there is hard.**
Both are real, and the second is the harder one. Worth researching before committing:

- **How to get data off the farm at all** — cellular coverage per location, or a long-range
  low-power radio network back to a gateway at the house. This is the gating constraint, not
  the sensors.
- **Cost per point**, installed, including power (solar? battery life?) and what it costs to
  maintain when something fails 40 minutes from the house.
- **What a level sensor buys us in pesos** — it should be justified against the decision it
  changes, the same as any other spend here.

_All of the above is unresearched. No figures until they're sourced._

## Backlog — 3D view modes *(raised 2026-08-21, not scheduled)*

Manuel's idea: switch between views the way Google Maps does — **default / satellite /
terrain** — and drape layers like contour lines over the 3D surface. Recorded with honest
effort estimates so it can be picked up cheaply later.

| Idea | Effort | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Toggle the orthophoto texture off** (plain shaded relief) | **Small — ~15 lines** | The shader already carries normalized height per vertex; it just needs a mode uniform |
| **Hypsometric terrain view** (elevation colour ramp + hillshade, no photo) | **Small** | Same mechanism as above. This is the "terrain" mode |
| **Drape contour lines on the 3D surface** | **Moderate — ~40 lines** | Look up elevation per vertex from the terrain grid, emit WebGL line geometry, lift slightly to avoid z-fighting with the surface |
| **Drape the other vector layers** (linderos, cercas, drenajes) | **Moderate** | Same machinery as contours — once one drapes, they all do |
| **Slope-shaded view** (colour by steepness) | Small–moderate | Genuinely useful, not just pretty: it would show at a glance which potreros cattle will under-graze |

**Assessment: the texture and terrain modes are an easy afternoon; draping vectors is a
slightly longer one.** Nothing here is hard — it is all the same mesh with different shading,
and the terrain grid is already in the page.

Worth saying which one earns its keep: **slope shading**. About 22% of the farm is steep
(15–25°), cattle under-graze steep ground, and seeing that against the potrero layout would
directly inform where to put fences and water. The rest is mostly pleasure — which is a fine
reason, just not the same reason.

## Design rules

Worth fixing now, before the thing grows:

1. **The dashboard renders; it never owns data.** Canonical data stays in the repo as CSV and
   GeoJSON. The page is a view. Delete it and lose nothing.
2. **Offline first.** It must work at the farm with no signal. That rules out anything needing
   a live tile server or a cloud service to draw a map.
3. **Stdlib only**, like the rest of the CLI. No build step, no npm, no framework. A single
   HTML file that opens in ten years.
4. **Recording must be faster than not recording.** Phase 2 lives or dies on this. If logging
   a move takes longer than doing the move, nobody logs it and the project is worthless.
5. **Never invent a number.** Same rule as everywhere else — a value with no provenance is a
   bug (see [`../AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md)).

## Where this sits against the rest of the work

Honestly: **below the P&L.** The operation is losing money monthly and the diagnosis is still
blocked on Manuel's cost and sale records
([`../data-inventory.md`](../data-inventory.md)). No dashboard fixes that.

But **Phase 1 and Phase 2 are cheap and they generate the operational data we've been missing
for months** — turnover, rest periods, per-potrero performance. So they're worth doing
alongside, not instead. Phase 4 costs real money and should wait until there's a P&L to
justify it against.

## Open questions

1. What sensors are at the house already, and how does their data come out — a local network,
   a vendor app, an API?
2. Is there any usable connectivity at the tanks and represas, or does everything have to
   come back to the house?
3. Who would actually record the cattle moves — Manuel, or the mayordomo? That decides
   whether this is a laptop tool or has to work on a phone, in the sun, with one hand.
4. Do the potreros have names everyone already uses? The 2003 plan's names (Bilbao, Siberia,
   Portugal, Marruecos…) are wonderful — are they still what people say?
